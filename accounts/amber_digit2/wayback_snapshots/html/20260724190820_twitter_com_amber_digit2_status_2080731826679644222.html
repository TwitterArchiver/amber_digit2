Reposting my analysis on the policing/carceral roots of the troubled teen industry (TTI) —privately run children’s torture and incarceration camps—in China. Due to parental and institutional support, the TTI grew significantly in the past two decades and has been overwhelmingly deployed against trans and queer children by parents seeking to “correct” their offspring’s “deviancy.”

Positioning the TTI as an institution allowing parents to outsource their violence/reproductive work, I argue that the family in post-1990 China has been thoroughly organized as an apparatus for population and mobility control endowed with policing duties as part of China’s transition towards a neoliberal economy, and the systemic domestic violence (家暴) that Chinese children endure should be understood as an extension of state violence (国/家暴).
