Contemporary prison abolitionism’s failures in providing any alternatives apart from 批斗会 (lynching) or 社区调解 (victim-blaming) are direct consequences of its empirical reduction of the prison from an institution of slavery that inaugurates liberal human subjectivity to a legible, empirical structure that can be isolated and eliminated without tackling the foundational violence of modernity named anti-blackness. The prison abolition movement thus forecloses revolutionary horizons by building a movement of institutional reparations through the plasticization of black flesh. See Teed (2023), “Whither Abolition” and Shackleford (2020), “Transformative Justice Can Only Exist If It’s Black”
