I am reposting here an announcement from Ripple Crisis Intervention Center, a suicide intervention center run entirely by volunteers in our community. It explains the conditions leading to the suicide of Sanqiu, a Chinese trans girl who passed away at the age of 17. Her death was the result of institutional negligence, severe domestic violence (including physical beatings, mental abuse), and denial of medical resources, along with profound depressive emotions and suspected psychotic symptoms. I hope everyone can read it closely to understand the dire circumstances attending to the mass deaths & suicide in Chinese transfem communities.

Seven days before Sanqiu’s death, after persuasion from our community suicide intervention workers, she agreed to go to the psychiatry department to get a prescription for psychiatric medication, but there was no specialized psychiatric hospital locally, and the medication prescription from the Chinese medicine hospital she went to cost over ¥1,000 (over $200) which extinguished the individual's will to seek treatment.

On July 24, her parents once again refused her (a minor) to seek psychiatric treatment, declaring that she had mental illness symptoms or need for psychiatric medications. On July 25, the individual in question made a suicide pact with another trans girl and was eventually discovered by her suicide intervention worker. The latter eventually for clues and physically tracked down the person, ultimately, with the assistance of other volunteers, escorted the individual and the other trans girl (who made the pact with her) to the police station.

Despite the severity of domestic violence, the police refused to provide assistance from shelters or other relief organizations. They even deported the victim from the city. On July 27, the individual committed suicide inside another police station.

Sanqiu was a trans girl who, subject to denial of medical resources and left completely vulnerable to domestic violence due to institutional negligence, has been thoroughly exhausted, with no remaining consciousness of resistance or desire for survival, only seeking death. In principle, her death may be prevented by police and community guidance to the family to have them hospitalized, addressing the illness and suicidal desires, but neither the parents nor the police realized the severity of the situation, repeatedly denying the individual’s pain and refusing to provide protective measures.

Sanqiu’s death was not a solitary case; it was one that exemplifies the material conditions of so many other trans girls living in China on the verge of survival, and of so many who have already passed away. I hope this case can provide everyone a glimpse into the devastating realities of trans girls in China and the possibility for building solidarity and care across borders.
